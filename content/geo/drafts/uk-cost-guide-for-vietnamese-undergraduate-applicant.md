# The Real Cost of Studying in the UK for Vietnamese Students (2026/27)
## Short answer
For a Vietnamese undergraduate, the cost of a UK degree breaks into four parts: **tuition, living costs, the Student visa fee, and the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS)**. As a realistic planning range for 2026/27, expect international tuition of roughly **£33,000–£38,000 a year** for a science subject like Computer Science, plus living costs the Home Office sets at **£1,171/month outside London** or **£1,529/month in London**. A one-off **£558 visa fee** and **£776/year IHS** are paid when you apply.

## Who this guide is for
This is for Vietnamese students and families budgeting for a UK undergraduate degree, who want real figures from official sources rather than rough estimates — so you can plan funds, scholarships, and the visa application with confidence.

## What to know first
The UK splits your costs into money paid to the university and money you must prove to the Home Office:

- **Tuition (paid to the university).** International fees vary by subject and university. For Computer Science in 2026/27, the [University of Manchester](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/courses/2026/00560/bsc-computer-science/) charges £37,800 and the [University of Nottingham](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/fees/tuitionfees/202627/undergraduate.aspx) £33,000 — a good guide to the science-subject range.
- **Living costs (you must prove these).** For a Student visa you must show **£1,529/month in London** or **£1,171/month outside London**, for up to nine months — so up to **£13,761** (London) or **£10,539** (outside) on top of your fees. Your actual spending may be higher.
- **Student visa fee.** **£558** when applying from outside the UK.
- **Immigration Health Surcharge.** **£776 per year** of your visa, paid upfront — for a three-year degree that is about **£2,328**, which then gives you access to the NHS.

| IHS (3-year course, paid upfront) | £2,328 |

1. **Confirm the exact tuition** on your course's official page — fees differ by university and subject, and some rise each year.
2. **Evidence the living-cost minimum** in an eligible account for the required period before you apply (see the official money rules below).
3. **Budget the visa fee and full IHS as upfront costs**, since both are paid with the application.
4. **Reduce the gap with scholarships** — many universities offer international merit awards, and government schemes exist for postgraduates.
5. **Plan for years two and three**: tuition plus living costs repeat annually (and fees can increase), even though the visa and IHS are mostly paid once.

## Best options by student type
- **Budget-sensitive family:** favour universities with confirmed, mid-range fees (e.g., Nottingham at £33,000) and cities outside London, where the living-cost requirement is lower.
- **London-set student:** budget the higher £1,529/month living requirement and look hard for scholarships to offset it.
- **Scholarship-seeker:** check each university's international undergraduate awards early; deadlines often fall alongside the application.

## How to choose
Compare universities on **total cost to graduation**, not just headline fee: multiply annual tuition by the course length, add three years of realistic living costs, and include the one-off visa and IHS. A confirmed fee in a lower-cost city can be far cheaper over three years than a slightly cheaper headline number elsewhere. Always verify the current figures on official pages before budgeting.

## FAQs
### How much money do I need to show for a UK Student visa?
Your course fees for the first year (as stated on your CAS) plus living costs of £1,529/month in London or £1,171/month outside London, for up to nine months. See the official money rules below.

### Is the health surcharge really paid upfront?
Yes. The IHS is £776 for each year of your visa and is paid in full when you apply, after which you can use the NHS like a UK resident.

### Can scholarships cover these costs?
Partly. Undergraduate international scholarships are usually partial (a tuition discount). Fully funded awards are more common at master's level — see Glowbal's scholarship guides.
